The Situation

How it started

When the business went into liquidation, the personal guarantee I had signed came straight to the front. I had always known it was there — but while the company was trading, it had never felt real. The moment it closed, it became very real. The creditor moved quickly and aggressively, threatening personal bankruptcy for both me and my co-director.

There was also five years of personal credit card and loan debt sitting alongside it — debt I had taken on partly to support the business when things were difficult. I hadn’t really separated the two in my mind. Suddenly both were pressing at once.

It felt like a living nightmare. I felt anxious and stressed in a way I hadn’t expected. It was affecting everything — job interviews weren’t going well, and every knock-back fed the fear and the guilt. I knew we had to get things under control.

What We Did and Why It Worked

The work behind the outcome

Mr. C’s personal guarantee was the immediate pressure point. The creditor was threatening bankruptcy action, which made this the most urgent strand of what was a complex overall picture. Our first task was to understand the guarantee — its terms, its enforceability, and what the creditor actually wanted.

We identified that a voluntary charge on the family home could give the creditor the security they needed to stand down from formal proceedings. This is not a route that suits every situation — but here, where the alternative was CCJ proceedings and a bankruptcy petition, it was a structured resolution that worked for both sides. The creditor accepted it. The bankruptcy threat was removed.

Alongside the guarantee, we addressed the personal debts — over £50,000 of credit card and loan liabilities that had been compounding for years. We brought everything into a single coordinated Creditor Arrangement, took over all creditor communication, and built the plan with flexibility for Mr. C’s new business. The co-director’s 50% share of the guaranteed debt was structured separately to reflect his different asset position. Mr. and Mrs. C now have a clear path forward. Their only regret was not coming to us sooner.

Creditor Arrangements — What You Need to Know

A Creditor Arrangement is a structured, negotiated repayment plan agreed directly between you and your creditors. You make the payments yourself — to your creditors — on terms that have been negotiated to reflect what you can genuinely afford.

Unlike a Debt Management Plan, where a third party collects and distributes your payment on your behalf, a Creditor Arrangement keeps you in control throughout. The payments are yours, the decisions are yours, and the arrangement adapts as your circumstances change.

The practical difference comes down to control. With a Debt Management Plan (DMP), you pay a third-party company, which then distributes the money to your creditors. You are relying on that company to handle your money and your relationships with creditors.

With a Creditor Arrangement, Lightside negotiates the terms on your behalf — but you make the payments directly to each creditor yourself. You remain in control of your money.

A voluntary charge is a legal agreement where a homeowner grants a creditor security over their property in exchange for agreed terms — typically more manageable repayments or protection from formal enforcement action such as a County Court Judgment (CCJ).

It is voluntary in the sense that it is negotiated and agreed, not imposed by a court. Used correctly, a voluntary charge can be a tool that protects both parties: the creditor has security, and the debtor avoids the disruption and costs of CCJ proceedings. It is not a step to take without professional advice — but in the right circumstances it can resolve what would otherwise be a difficult impasse.
